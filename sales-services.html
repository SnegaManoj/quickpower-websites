Air Atomizing Spray Nozzles

Quickpower provides information about air atomizing nozzles, which use compressed air to break a liquid stream into fine droplets. Available designs include flat and round spray patterns, with atomization affected by air flow and nozzle configuration.

About Air Atomizing Spray Nozzles

These nozzles combine compressed air and liquid to produce a controlled spray. Available configurations include pressure, siphon and gravity-head feed methods, as well as internal- and external-mixing designs.

Working principles and feed methods

Pressure principle

Liquid is supplied under pressure, for example by a pump or pressurized container, with a separate compressed-air supply.

Siphon principle

Airflow lifts liquid from a lower level. The catalogue describes this for installations without a pump or pressurized liquid container.

Gravity head principle

Liquid reaches the nozzle by gravity from an elevated supply, where a pump or pressurized container is not used.

Mixing and spray patterns

Internal mixing

Air and liquid meet inside the nozzle and exit through the same orifice. The catalogue describes very fine atomization; changing air flow also affects liquid flow. It notes internal-mix setups are generally unsuitable above 200 centipoise viscosity.

External mixing

Air and liquid leave separately and mix outside the nozzle. Their flow rates can be controlled independently. The catalogue notes these setups can be used with liquids above 200 centipoise and abrasive suspensions.

Spray patterns

The catalogue includes flat fan patterns and round patterns, including full-cone designs. Pattern, coverage and atomization depend on the selected nozzle and operating conditions.

External mix operation

Air and liquid leave the nozzle through separate passages and combine outside it. Since the streams are not connected within the nozzle, their flow rates can be controlled independently, allowing precise liquid metering. Increasing air flow produces finer atomization.

Pressure principle

In a pressure-feed arrangement, liquid is supplied under pressure by a pump or pressurized container, with a separate compressed-air supply. This is a general feed principle; the nozzle information above identifies a siphon-fed setup.
