How Does a Tensile Testing Machine Determine Breaking Point?

An overview of how tensile testing helps identify material strength and failure behaviour.

View it →
  • A prepared specimen is secured between the machine’s grips.
  • The machine pulls the specimen at a controlled rate.
  • A load cell measures the force applied during the test.
  • The system records how far the specimen extends, producing a load–extension curve.
  • Fracture is identified when the specimen breaks, typically with a sudden drop in measured load.
  • The highest load before fracture is the maximum tensile load; tensile strength is calculated using the specimen’s original cross-sectional area.

Results depend on the specimen, test method and machine setup; no product rating is implied.

Salt Spray Test Procedure According to ASTM B117 Standards

Explore the purpose and procedure of salt spray testing for corrosion evaluation.

View it →
  • Prepare a salt solution and atomize it to create a fine fog inside the chamber.
  • Place prepared specimens in the chamber so they are exposed to the fog without touching one another.
  • Maintain the chamber conditions and salt-fog collection as specified by the applicable test procedure.
  • Expose specimens for the duration selected by the test plan; ASTM B117 does not set a universal exposure time or pass/fail rating.
  • After exposure, handle and assess the specimens using the agreed inspection procedure.
  • Record observations such as corrosion or coating changes and compare them against the project’s acceptance criteria.

Follow the current ASTM B117 edition and the applicable product instructions for the exact method and setup.

How Cyclic Corrosion Chambers Simulate Real Conditions

Learn how changing exposure cycles are used to simulate varied environments.

View it →
  • Cycle through selected exposure stages, such as salt spray, humidity and drying, according to the test method.
  • Apply each stage for its specified duration to reproduce changing environmental conditions.
  • Repeat the sequence to observe how repeated wetting and drying affect materials and protective coatings.
  • Use the chamber’s controlled cycle sequence to make tests more consistent and repeatable.
  • Inspect specimens at defined intervals or after the test to document corrosion and coating changes.
  • Compare results using the applicable test method and project acceptance criteria.

Available stages, settings and cycle programs depend on the chamber model and selected test method.
